# Ghana SHS Core Courses — Review Draft

**Status:** For review; not published to the course catalogue
**Audience:** SHS 1–3
**Scope:** The four SHS core subjects currently represented in the app's learning catalogue: English Language (Core), Core Mathematics, Integrated Science, and Social Studies.

This is an original course outline and practice draft organized around Ghanaian SHS learning and WASSCE preparation. It is not a verbatim copy of an official syllabus. Before publishing, check unit names, term sequencing, and required depth against the current NaCCA curriculum and WAEC syllabus used by the school.

Each course can be delivered as short lessons, guided examples, locally relevant activities, independent practice, and periodic revision. The SHS 1–3 sequence below is a suggested progression; schools may adjust it to their approved scheme of learning.

The Core Mathematics outline has been expanded into a separate [full lesson draft](./SHS_Core_Mathematics_Full_Lessons_Draft.md). The other three subjects remain outlines pending review.

## 1. English Language (Core)

### Course outcomes

Students should be able to read a range of texts critically, write clearly for different audiences and purposes, use standard English accurately, summarize information in their own words, and communicate confidently in speech.

### Suggested unit sequence

| Level | Units |
|---|---|
| SHS 1 | Reading for main ideas and evidence; paragraph unity and development; parts of speech and sentence patterns; narrative and descriptive writing; vocabulary in context; listening and clear speech |
| SHS 2 | Argumentative and expository writing; formal and informal letters; comprehension and inference; summary skills; clauses, agreement, tense, and punctuation; oral English sounds and stress |
| SHS 3 | Timed essay planning and revision; integrated comprehension and summary practice; tone, register, and audience; lexis and structure; oral English review; mock examination and feedback |

### Worked examples

1. **Subject–verb agreement:** “Each of the players ___ ready.” The correct choice is **is**. The subject is “each,” which is singular; “of the players” does not change the subject.
2. **Concise summary:** Original: “The assembly members organized a clean-up, cleared blocked drains, and spoke with residents about disposing of rubbish properly.” Summary: **The assembly promoted sanitation through a community clean-up and public education.** The summary keeps the central idea and uses fresh wording.

### Try work

1. Choose the correct verb: “Neither the teacher nor the students ___ late.” *(was / were)*
2. Rewrite with correct punctuation: `ama said we should meet at the library on friday`
3. Change to passive voice: “The science club planted trees around the school.”
4. Write a formal letter to your headteacher requesting more books for the school library. State two reasons and propose one practical step.
5. Plan an argumentative essay on: **“Community service should be part of every SHS student's education.”** Give a thesis and three supporting points.
6. In 2–3 sentences, summarize this passage: “After heavy rain, water collected beside the market because plastic bags blocked the drains. Traders and students removed the rubbish, and the assembly arranged regular collection. The community also agreed to keep waste out of the gutters.”
7. Explain the difference between the writer's **main claim** and a **supporting detail** in a passage of your choice.
8. Read a short paragraph aloud, mark its stressed words, and explain how changing the stress can change the speaker's meaning.

**Review notes:** The teacher's guide should include a model response and a brief marking checklist for writing tasks; open-ended responses should allow more than one defensible answer.

## 2. Core Mathematics

### Course outcomes

Students should be able to select and apply appropriate mathematical methods, show working clearly, interpret quantitative information, and solve problems drawn from everyday Ghanaian contexts.

### Suggested unit sequence

| Level | Units |
|---|---|
| SHS 1 | Number operations, fractions, ratios, percentages, and rates; sets; algebraic expressions and linear equations; angles and plane shapes; measurement; tables, charts, and averages |
| SHS 2 | Indices and standard form; simultaneous equations and inequalities; functions and graphs; similarity and scale; area, volume, and surface area; probability; spread of data |
| SHS 3 | Sequences and patterns; coordinate geometry and transformations; trigonometry and bearings; compound measures and finance; statistics and probability; mixed WASSCE-style problem solving |

### Worked examples

1. **Linear equation:** Solve `3x − 5 = 16`. Add 5 to both sides: `3x = 21`. Divide by 3: **`x = 7`**. Check: `3(7) − 5 = 16`.
2. **Mean and median:** Find the mean and median of `48, 52, 55, 60, 65`. The sum is 280, so the mean is `280 ÷ 5 = 56`. The middle value is **55**, so the median is 55.

### Try work

1. A 2 kg bag of rice costs GH₵38. Find the cost of 5 kg at the same rate.
2. A shirt marked GH₵160 is discounted by 15%. Find the sale price.
3. Solve: `5x + 7 = 32`.
4. Solve simultaneously: `x + y = 13` and `x − y = 3`.
5. A rectangular classroom is 8 m long and 6 m wide. Find its area and perimeter.
6. A fair die is rolled once. Find the probability of getting a number greater than 4.
7. Find the mean of `6, 8, 8, 10, 13`.
8. A trader invests GH₵2,400 at 10% simple interest per year for 2 years. Find the interest and total amount.

**Answer check:** 1. GH₵95; 2. GH₵136; 3. `x = 5`; 4. `x = 8, y = 5`; 5. 48 m² and 28 m; 6. `1/3`; 7. 9; 8. GH₵480 interest and GH₵2,880 total.

## 3. Integrated Science

### Course outcomes

Students should be able to ask testable questions, plan fair investigations, interpret evidence, explain scientific ideas, and connect science to health, technology, agriculture, and the Ghanaian environment.

### Suggested unit sequence

| Level | Units |
|---|---|
| SHS 1 | Scientific inquiry and measurement; matter and mixtures; cells and organization; nutrition and health; forces and motion; energy forms; ecosystems and local habitats |
| SHS 2 | Atomic structure and chemical change; electricity and simple circuits; heat and energy transfer; human body systems; reproduction and inheritance; soil, crops, and food production; water and sanitation |
| SHS 3 | Acids, bases, and everyday materials; waves and applications; disease prevention and public health; climate, pollution, and conservation; experimental design and data; integrated WASSCE revision |

### Worked examples

1. **Density:** A stone has a mass of 540 g and a volume of 200 cm³. Density is `mass ÷ volume = 540 ÷ 200 =` **2.7 g/cm³**.
2. **Series circuit:** A 6 V battery is connected to 2 Ω and 4 Ω resistors in series. Total resistance is `2 + 4 = 6 Ω`. Current is `V ÷ R = 6 ÷ 6 =` **1 A**. The voltage across the 4 Ω resistor is `I × R = 1 × 4 =` **4 V**.

### Try work

1. Name one variable to change, one to measure, and two to keep constant when investigating how light affects seedling growth.
2. A liquid has a mass of 150 g and occupies 125 cm³. Calculate its density.
3. Give one physical method for separating sand from water and explain why it works.
4. State two functions of the human respiratory system.
5. A 12 V supply is connected to a 3 Ω resistor. Calculate the current.
6. Describe one way blocked drains can increase health risks in a community, and suggest one preventive action.
7. Explain the difference between a food chain and a food web.
8. A student repeats an experiment three times and gets different readings. Give two steps to improve the reliability of the results.

**Answer check:** 2. 1.2 g/cm³; 3. Filtration, because the sand particles are retained by the filter while water passes through; 5. 4 A. Other answers should be checked for scientific accuracy and a clear link between evidence and explanation.

## 4. Social Studies

### Course outcomes

Students should be able to explain how individuals and institutions shape Ghanaian society, assess social and environmental issues using evidence, understand civic rights and responsibilities, and propose practical, inclusive responses to community challenges.

### Suggested unit sequence

| Level | Units |
|---|---|
| SHS 1 | Ghanaian identity and shared values; family, school, and community; citizenship, rights, and responsibilities; physical environment and resources; population and settlement; basic research and use of evidence |
| SHS 2 | Governance and democratic participation; national development and employment; entrepreneurship and household decisions; culture, diversity, and social cohesion; urbanization and sanitation; conflict prevention and resolution |
| SHS 3 | Public policy and accountability; sustainable use of resources; regional and global relationships; media and responsible citizenship; analysis of contemporary Ghanaian case studies; WASSCE essay and source-based revision |

### Worked examples

1. **Community sanitation case:** A market's drains repeatedly become blocked. Identify causes (poor disposal and irregular collection), effects (flooding and health risks), stakeholders (traders, residents, assembly, and waste services), and a response (reliable collection plus community education and enforcement). A strong answer connects each proposal to an identified cause.
2. **Household budgeting case:** A household's food and transport costs rise while income stays the same. A useful response is to list essential and non-essential spending, compare prices, set priorities, and review the budget. The answer should explain trade-offs rather than assume that every expense can be eliminated.

### Try work

1. Give two rights and two responsibilities of a Ghanaian citizen.
2. Explain two ways local government can support development in a district.
3. State two causes and two effects of rural–urban migration in Ghana.
4. Describe one way a community can protect a local water body from pollution.
5. Explain how peaceful elections strengthen democratic governance.
6. A community has limited funds for either a borehole repair or a new meeting centre. Give two criteria residents could use to set priorities fairly.
7. Identify one possible benefit and one possible risk of social media in public discussion.
8. Choose a current development issue in Ghana. State the issue, identify two affected groups, and propose one realistic action for each group.

**Review notes:** Encourage evidence-based responses, awareness of regional differences, and consideration of who benefits or bears costs. Avoid treating one community's experience as representative of all Ghana.

## Suggested course-page pattern

For each unit, prepare:

1. A short lesson with clear learning objectives and key vocabulary.
2. At least two worked or model examples, progressing from guided to independent work.
3. A short Ghana-relevant activity, data set, text, or case study.
4. Eight or more practice questions, mixing recall, explanation, application, and extended response.
5. An answer guide with worked steps for calculations and marking points for open-ended work.
6. A short formative quiz and a revision task connecting the unit to earlier learning.
